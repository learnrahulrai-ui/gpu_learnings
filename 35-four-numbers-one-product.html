The story

In the Lab 6 prelab each of the 32 threads loads 4 numbers of A, 2 of B and 4 of C, and then all 32 execute one tensor core instruction. Every number of the result D is a sum of 8 products, one for each of 8 values of k, plus a number of C.

The learner could not make the counts agree. A thread holds 4 numbers of A, yet one number of D needs 8 of them; it holds 2 numbers of B, yet 8 are needed; and a thread is supposed to compute only with its own registers. To the learner the result looked like it appeared from nowhere, and the instruction looked as if it could not even know which thread it was executing in.

Where do the other numbers come from when one thread's D is computed, and which threads supply them?

Tempting answer that fails: "each thread multiplies its own 4 numbers of A by its own 2 of B".
True by coincidence for D(0, 0), whose numbers all sit in threads 0 to 3 (L04); false for
D(0, 1), whose B numbers sit in threads 4 to 7 (L05), and for 7 of every 8 values of j (L06).

In plain words

Each of the 32 threads brings a few numbers, and one instruction for all of them multiplies the whole matrices that the 32 threads hold together. A single number of the result is built from numbers brought by several different threads, so no thread on its own could compute it. The threads only load and store; the multiplication of the whole small matrices happens once, for all of them.

For a manager: a count that looks impossible for one thread can be exact for the 32 together; ask in which unit a count is made before deciding that work is missing.
