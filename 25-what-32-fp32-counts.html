25. Lecture 2: what 32 FP32 exec units count, and how many floats a thread can hold

The story

Lecture 2 of MIT 6.S894 describes one "core" of the course GPU, an NVIDIA RTX 4000 Ada. NVIDIA calls a set of 32 threads that execute one instruction together a warp, and the circuit that picks which warp's next instruction begins a warp scheduler; the slide's core is one warp scheduler, with 32 exec units for 32-bit numbers, 512 registers of 32 times 32 bits, one instruction for a warp per clock, and up to 12 warps kept at once.

The learner heard FP32, warp scheduler and one instruction per clock together and could not connect them. A thread can hold thousands of float variables, so how can there be only one exec unit per thread? If each of 32 threads computes three independent sums, or fifteen, does some of the work leak into another thread's hardware? And what do these numbers look like on the laptop's GTX 1650?

What does a count of exec units limit: how many floats a thread can have, or how many float operations can begin in one clock?

Connect the numbers

L15 DO Collect L03 (32 additions per clock, one per thread), L04 (3 sums in 32 threads: 3 instructions, 3 clocks; thread 5 always on unit 5), L07 (15 sums: 30 cycles on the laptop), L08 (a chain of 15: about 60 cycles, units busy half the time), L11 (64 to 255 registers per thread, the rest in memory) and L12 (32 times fewer FP64 results than FP32 per clock on the laptop). Twist from these numbers: if each of 32 threads holds 1,000 float variables, can the course GPU's scheduler still begin 32 additions per clock? ____ . Write one sentence on what "32 x 32-bit exec units" counts. from: L03, L04, L07, L08, L11, L12

CHECK 
Yes, whenever the values each addition needs are in registers and ready: the 1,000
variables live mostly in memory (L11) and cost loads, but they do not change how many
additions can begin per clock.

"32 x 32-bit exec units" counts the 32-bit float operations that can begin per clock,
one for each thread of a warp; it says nothing about how many floats a thread may hold
(registers, then memory), and nothing about doubles, which use other units (L12).

Tempting answer that fails: "one exec unit per thread means one float per thread". L04's
3 sums and L05's 15 sums are 3 and 15 instructions on the same units, and L11 gives each
thread 64 to 255 registers before memory.

In plain words

On a graphics chip, one control circuit gives the same instruction to 32 threads, and 32 arithmetic circuits carry it out, one per thread. That count says how many number operations begin per clock tick, not how many numbers a thread can keep: a thread may have thousands of numbers, held in fast storage and in memory, and they take turns on the arithmetic circuits.

For a manager: a chip's count of arithmetic units sets its peak rate for one kind of number; the same chip can be 32 times slower on another kind, and how much it can hold is a separate figure.

The interviewer's next question

On the laptop, the 15-add chain of L08 leaves a scheduler's FP32 units idle half the time. Without changing the arithmetic, how many warps per scheduler fill that idle half, and do registers limit it?

CHECK 
Each add waits 4 cycles and needs 2 cycles to begin, so 4 / 2 = 2 warps per scheduler keep
the units busy: one begins its add while the other waits.
Registers: 16,384 per scheduler / (2 warps * 32 threads) = 256 per thread, above the
255 limit, so registers do not limit 2 warps; with 8 warps, the most a laptop scheduler
holds, a thread may use at most 64 (L11).
