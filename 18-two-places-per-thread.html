The story

Lab 2 Part 1 on the GPU keeps the launch of the Lab 1 kernel, a single block of 32 threads, and asks each thread to handle two places of the picture at once instead of one. The reason comes straight from the prelab: on this laptop, two independent chains of multiply-adds got through twice as many multiply-adds per cycle as one chain. A thread with two places has two independent chains. The obvious prediction was close to half the time.

The learner first had to get the indexing right, and said exactly where the thinking broke: there were two ideas colliding, one about the step of 32 or 64 and one about two places, and it was not clear why the step should double when each thread still moves by one set of places at a time. Then came two working versions. Both gave exactly the right picture. Neither was faster.

The baseline already has 32 threads working on 32 different places, all independent of each other. What can a second place inside the same thread give the hardware that those 32 independent places do not?

All 32 threads wait, because they are executing one and the same instruction; 32 different

the wait: from another warp, or from a second independent place inside the same thread.
Here that second place had little to fill: the baseline's 9 FP32 instructions already take
18 cycles to begin against a 16-cycle chain, so the second place mostly added starts
(1.79 instead of 1 per repetition), while sets of 64 loop until their slowest place
(0.622 instead of 0.5). Version 1 lost a further factor of about 2 to control instructions.

Tempting answer that fails: "two independent chains doubled the rate in the prelab, so two
places per thread should give about 111 ms". In the prelab a lone chain left half of every
4-cycle wait empty; this loop's wait was already almost covered by its own starts (L11).

In plain words

Giving each worker two jobs at once only helps if the worker spends time waiting that the second job can fill. Here the GPU was already busy beginning instructions for almost the whole wait, so a second job per thread mostly added work. A first attempt that checked "is this job still going?" around every step was twice as slow, because the checks cost more than the arithmetic.

For a manager: a technique that doubled speed in a small test can do nothing in the real job. Ask what the small test was limited by, and whether the real job is limited by the same thing.
