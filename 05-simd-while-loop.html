05. SIMD and the while loop: eight iters, one loop

Each line uses only what the lines above it made. The grey note says which line it takes from. Fill every ____ on paper, then open CHECK.

The story

Each place of the picture needs its own number of repetitions, anywhere from 1 to 1000. The AVX2 instructions handle 8 places at once, but an instruction cannot do something to some of its 8 numbers and something else to the others. So how can one loop give 8 places 8 different numbers of repetitions?

The learner attacked this from every side. SIMD does not understand while loops. SIMD cannot be told to update some numbers and not others. There is no way to know in advance which of the 8 will finish first. All three statements are true, and the solution does not contradict any of them.

The learner's three statements are the constraints. Any solution has to respect all of them at once and still give every one of the 8 places its correct count. Before reading the code, try to say what such a solution would have to waste.

Your words: "SIMD does not even understand while loop. So where is the connection between this while loop and SIMD?" / "How can I tell SIMD that don't update 8, 9 but only update 10, 11, 12?" / "Suppose one square finishes after two repetitions, but another needs five. There is no way to know." / "It just happens that the mask contains negative numbers, right?"

In plain words

Doing 8 things with one instruction only pays when the 8 things need the same amount of work. The vector code here gets about 7 times faster than plain code because neighbouring parts of this picture happen to need similar work. Pick the 8 badly and the same hardware gives barely 2 times.

For a manager: hardware speedups quoted as "8 times" or "32 times" are maximums. The real number depends on how similar the units of work are, and that is a property of your data, not of the chip.
