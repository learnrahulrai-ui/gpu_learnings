40. Triton 1397: an 8-bit A converted before the multiply, half the bytes and 44% slower

The story

Triton is a language for writing GPU kernels in Python, and its tutorial includes a matrix multiplication. In March 2023 a user changed one thing in it: matrix A was stored as 8-bit integers instead of 16-bit floats, and converted to 16-bit floats inside the loop just before the multiply. The report, Triton issue 1397, is still open.

The learner found the result absurd. The 8-bit A moves half as many bytes, and a conversion is one cheap instruction per number, yet the kernel lost almost half its speed. Worse, the explanation offered was that the loads were no longer started ahead of the computation, as if one conversion could change how a kernel loads its data.

How can converting each number, which costs almost nothing, make a kernel that moves fewer bytes slower?

In plain words

Storing half of the data in a smaller format should make a computation faster, since less data moves. Here it made it slower, because the small numbers and the converted numbers have to sit in different places inside the hardware, and the tool could not keep loading the next pieces in advance while it rearranged them. The cost of a step lies in what it forces around it, not in the step itself.

For a manager: a change that looks free can switch off an optimization; ask whether a small change alters how data reaches the hardware before assuming its cost is small.

The interviewer's next question

Why store A as 8-bit integers at all? For A of 4096 by 4096 numbers, compute the bytes in 8 bits and in 16 bits, and say what a fix has to keep and what it has to change.

CHECK 
4096 * 4096 = 16,777,216 numbers: 16.8 MB in 8 bits, 33.6 MB in 16 bits. The 8-bit storage
halves the memory and the bytes read for A, which is the reason to keep it. A fix keeps 8-bit
storage and changes how the numbers reach the 16-bit layout: rearranging them on the way, in
shared memory or in registers, without stopping the early loads. The reporter proposed a route
through shared memory; the maintainer later wrote that the int8 slowness itself was not yet
addressed.
