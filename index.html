Why this exists

Anyone can now produce unlimited text about GPUs. Definitions of SIMD, threads and memory are a search away, and a person can recite all of them and still be unable to finish a lab. This site is built from the opposite material: the exact places where one learner, working through MIT course 6.S894 on a laptop, got stuck, argued, and was wrong or right, recorded from their own chats with ChatGPT.

The learner's own diagnosis of the gap was blunt: anyone can say what SIMD is, anyone can say the work is done in parallel; the hard part is connecting the pieces precisely. So every page here has three layers. A story, in plain sentences, says what the problem is and where the learner's thinking broke. A set of numbered lines makes you do the arithmetic yourself, with the real numbers from the learner's machine, each line using only what came before it. And a final puzzle collects five to ten of those results and asks for the one meaning behind them, which no definition supplies.

The calculations are not the point; any careful person can do them. The point is the step after: putting several results side by side and seeing what they say together. That step is what gets tested in a good interview and what stays after the course is forgotten.

How to read it

If you have only just heard of GPUs, or you lead a team and want to know what matters: read each page's story at the top and its "In plain words" box at the bottom. Begin with page 00.

If you write code: do every DO line with pencil and paper before opening CHECK. The numbers are real, from a Ryzen 5 4600H and a GTX 1650.

If code is new to you: every piece of code has a panel under it that reads it in English, line by line, and every technical word is defined in plain English on the word list.

If you want the reasoning workout: do the "Connect the numbers" box on each page before reading its CHECK. Each CHECK names the tempting answer that fails and the line that refutes it.

  19. A second place per thread on the GPU: your 461 ms and 231 ms against a 208 ms baseline, the two indexing traps, and why 32 independent places do not fill a wait.
