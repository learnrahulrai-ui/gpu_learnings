GPU Learnings: do it, don't read it

Made from one real chat working through MIT 6.S894 Lab 1 (Mandelbrot on CPU, CPU SIMD, GPU) and Lab 2, on a Ryzen 5 4600H and a GTX 1650.

Each page begins from zero. Each line uses only what the lines above it made, and a grey note names the line it takes from. Each page quotes the exact place the learner got stuck, then hands you real numbers to calculate. The 65536 iters values used here add up to 16274722, the same "CPU total" the machine printed. Pencil and paper. Fill every ____ before you open CHECK.

  1. From (i, j) to the middleman pair (cx, cy): where 2.5 and -2.0 come from, and why (9, 7) gives different (cx, cy) for different img_size.
  2. The while loop by hand: why x2, y2, w begin at 0, an x2 + y2 that goes down before the loop is left, and iters 1 versus iters 3 for the same (9, 7).
  3. The w line: the lab code never writes 2*x*y; derive it, and connect it to your I times I = -1.
  4. SIMD: eight cx values from one number: the offsets 0 to 7 forced out of the cx line, the inputs of fmadd, and the argument-order trap.
  5. SIMD and the while loop: eight iters in one loop, a mask that reads as -1, and 7.11 predicted against 7.07 measured.
  6. GPU: one changed line, and the proof of "nearby": the profiler's 2,048 and 8,192 derived by hand, and 22.06 predicted against 22.75 measured.
  7. Lab 2: which line waits for which line: a chain of 5 inside 9 ops, and 11 cycles with nothing beginning.
  8. Lab 2: when does the fat while loop stop?: wrong stopping tests, the right one, and more useless work for fewer cycles.
