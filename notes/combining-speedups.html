Combining speedups

Topics: measuring. A single idea, with a few lines to calculate. Each line uses only the lines above it, and the grey note names its source. Fill every ____ on paper, then open CHECK. Any unfamiliar word: words used on this site.

The idea

Lab 2 combines several techniques and asks whether their speedups carry over when combined. Multiplying the speedups of the steps from the first version to the last, and checking that the product matches the total, feels rigorous.

The question here: can that check ever fail, and which comparison does say whether a technique still helps?

Work it out

L01 Illustrative times from the Lab 2 tutor file, not measured: B (baseline) = 120 ms; I (B plus independent sets of work inside each thread) = 60; C (many SMs) = 24; T (many SMs and more threads per SM) = 12; F (T plus independent sets) = 10. from: Lab 2 tutor file

L02 DO (B / C) x (C / T) x (T / F) = ____ x ____ x ____ = ____ . B / F = ____ . Now any other times, B = 97, C = 41, T = 13, F = 7: product = ____ , B / F = ____ . Write the product as one fraction and cancel: ____ . from: L01

CHECK 
5 x 2 x 1.2 = 12 = B / F
97/41 x 41/13 x 13/7 = 97/7 = 13.86 = B / F
(B x C x T) / (C x T x F) = B / F for any times: the check cannot fail, so it tests nothing

L03 DO Gain of the independent sets alone = B / I = ____ . Gain of the same sets on top of T = T / F = ____ . from: L01

CHECK 
alone: 120 / 60 = 2
on top of T: 12 / 10 = 1.2 -> the gain shrank

L04 Measured on your GTX 1650, cycles per multiply-add: 4 with one chain, 2 with two independent chains, 2 with four. from: your output

L05 DO Gain from 1 to 2 chains = ____ . From 2 to 4 chains = ____ . Fill: independent sets inside a thread and more threads per scheduler both fill the same ____ , so once one has filled it, the other gains ____ . from: L03, L04

CHECK 
1 to 2 chains: 2 times; 2 to 4 chains: 1 time (no gain)
both fill the same waiting cycles; once they are filled, the other has nothing left to fill
The test of combining is B / I against T / F (L03), never the step-by-step product (L02).
