The story

Part 3 of Lab 2 puts more than one thread on each CPU core and more than one set of 32 threads on each GPU scheduler; part 4 adds the independent sets of part 1 back in. The lab asks how much each step helps, which number of threads is best, and what decides it.

Each technique, tried alone, gives a speedup, and the natural expectation is that the speedups multiply. The learner asked what would make someone who had memorized everything still fail at this point.

What do the three techniques have in common, and how do you know when adding more of one stops paying?

Guess first

On the laptop's GPU, the Lab 2 loop with one set of 32 threads per scheduler: guess how many sets per scheduler are worth having before more stop helping: 1, about 2, or 8 or more?

Write your guess on paper now, before any calculation on this page.

Your words: "What will make a student fail in each of the parts of the assignment due to memorization? Let's say there is somebody who has memorized all the things but still cannot reason it out."

Work it out

L01 Laptop facts: the CPU has 6 cores with 2 hardware threads each, 12 in all; the GPU has 56 schedulers, each holding up to 8 warps (NVIDIA's name for a set of 32 consecutive threads), 448 in all. A scheduler begins at most one instruction per cycle; a 32-thread FP32 instruction takes 2 cycles to begin; an FP32 operation that needs the previous result waits 4 cycles. from: your hardware table, NVIDIA Turing Tuning Guide, your output (Lab 2 prelab)

L02 The Lab 2 loop has 9 FP32 instructions per repetition and a repeating chain of 4 operations, 16 cycles. Measured on the laptop: the baseline, one block of 32 threads and so one warp, took about 208 ms for 8,792,320 warp-repetitions (one repetition of the loop by the whole warp). from: Lab 2 starter code, your listing, your output, computation on the Lab 2 iters

L03 DO Time per warp-repetition = 208 ms / 8,792,320 = ____ ns. At an assumed 1.5 billion cycles per second = ____ cycles. FP32 begins per repetition = 9 x 2 = ____ cycles; chain = ____ cycles. Cycles in which the scheduler begins no FP32 instruction, at least = ____ . from: L01, L02, assumption

CHECK 
208e-3 / 8,792,320 = 23.7 ns, x 1.5 = 35.5 cycles per repetition
begins 18 cycles, chain 16: neither explains 35.5
about 35.5 - 18 = 17.5 cycles per repetition with no FP32 begin: the compare, the branch, the
count, and the waits around them

L04 DO A second warp on the same scheduler can begin its instructions in those idle cycles. Warps per scheduler before the FP32 begins fill every cycle = 35.5 / ____ = ____ . Cycles of the scheduler per warp-repetition, with 1, 2 and 4 warps per scheduler, at best = ____ , ____ , ____ . from: L03

CHECK 
35.5 / 18 = about 2 warps per scheduler
1 warp: 35.5; 2 warps: max(35.5, 36) / 2 = 18 each; 4 warps: max(35.5, 72) / 4 = 18 each
Past about 2 warps per scheduler the FP32 begins are the limit, and more warps add nothing.
This rests on the assumed clock: measure it.

L05 The same mechanism on the CPU: the 2 hardware threads of one core share its arithmetic circuits, and either can begin instructions while the other waits. For one 8-place set, the core's chain takes 4 x 3 = 12 cycles per repetition and its 9 vector instructions take about 4.5 cycles to begin (published Zen 2 figures: a 3-cycle wait, at least 2 vector FP32 instructions begun per cycle). from: AMD SMT description, Agner Fog's instruction tables (Zen 2)

L06 DO If one CPU thread already carries 2 or 3 independent sets that fill the 12-cycle chain, what can a second hardware thread on the same core still fill? ____ . If each thread carries only one set, what does the second thread fill? ____ . from: L05

CHECK 
little: both threads then compete for the same begins
with one set per thread, the second thread fills the waiting that a second set would have filled:
the two techniques fill the same idle cycles

L07 Illustrative times from the Lab 2 tutor file, not measured: B (baseline) = 120 ms; I (B plus independent sets inside each thread) = 60; C (many SMs) = 24; T (many SMs and more threads per SM) = 12; F (T plus independent sets) = 10. from: Lab 2 tutor file

L08 DO (B / C) x (C / T) x (T / F) = ____ ; B / F = ____ . With any other times, B = 97, C = 41, T = 13, F = 7: product = ____ , B / F = ____ . Gain of the independent sets alone = B / I = ____ ; on top of T = T / F = ____ . from: L07

CHECK 
5 x 2 x 1.2 = 12 = B / F; 97/41 x 41/13 x 13/7 = 97/7 = 13.86 = B / F: C and T cancel for any
times, so the product cannot fail as a check
alone 2; on top of T 1.2: the gain shrank, because T had already filled the same waiting

L09 DO Registers: an SM has 65,536. A kernel with 72 registers per thread fits 65,536 / (72 x 32) = ____ warps per SM, ____ per scheduler. With L04's need of about 2 per scheduler, are registers the limit? ____ . At 128 registers: ____ per scheduler; the limit? ____ . from: L01, L04, NVIDIA Turing Tuning Guide

CHECK 
28.4 -> 28 warps, 7 per scheduler: no
128 registers: 65,536 / 4,096 = 16 warps, 4 per scheduler: still no
Registers limit a kernel only when the work needs more warps per scheduler than fit; read your
kernel's use with -Xptxas -v.

Connect the numbers

L13 DO Collect L03 (17.5 idle cycles per repetition), L04 (about 2 warps per scheduler, then flat), L06 (a second hardware thread fills the same waiting as a second set), L08 (the product always matches; 2 alone, 1.2 on top), L09 (registers are not the limit at 7 or 4 per scheduler). Write one sentence answering the lab's last question: why do techniques that each help alone gain little together, and how many threads are enough? from: L03, L04, L06, L08, L09

CHECK 
Independent sets inside a thread, more threads per core and more warps per scheduler all fill
the same idle cycles; enough is the count at which the begins fill them (here about 2 warps per
scheduler), and past it every technique adds work, not speed. Test combining with the gain on top
(T / F), never with the step-by-step product.

Tempting answer that fails: "use the most threads the hardware allows". L04 puts the useful
number near 2 per scheduler, against 8 held.

When it goes wrong

  • More threads per core made the CPU version slower. The threads compete for the same begins, and start-up costs grow with their number (L06).
  • The GPU stops improving at about 8 warps per block. Expected: about 2 per scheduler (L04).
  • The ILP version gains less on top of multithreading than alone. Both fill the same idle cycles (L08).
  • The "speedups multiply" check succeeds. It always succeeds, for any times (L08).

In plain words

The three techniques, more work inside each thread, more threads per core and more sets of threads per scheduler, sound different but solve the same problem: hardware waiting for results. Once the waiting is filled, none of them can help further, so combining them gives much less than multiplying their separate gains.

For a manager: when two optimizations each give 2 times, budget for far less than 4 until someone measures one on top of the other.

The interviewer's next question

Your measurements show the single-SM time still falling from 8 to 16 warps per block. Which assumption of L03 and L04 is the most likely to be wrong, and how do you check it?

CHECK 
The clock, or the count of instructions. A higher clock than 1.5 GHz means more cycles per
repetition and more idle cycles to fill; instructions other than FP32 (compares, integer adds,
branches) also take begins, so the true limit is lower than 18 cycles. Check the first by reading
the clock during the measurement (nvidia-smi -q -d CLOCK) and the second by counting every
instruction of the loop in the listing (cuobjdump), not only the FP32 ones.
