What the next iteration can begin without

Topics: Lecture 2, chains. A single idea, with a few lines to calculate. Each line uses only the lines above it, and the grey note names its source. Fill every ____ on paper, then open CHECK. Any unfamiliar word: words used on this site.

The idea

An iteration of the Lecture 2 loop cannot finish until its two values arrive from memory, hundreds of cycles after the loads begin. A processor that may begin an instruction as soon as its inputs are ready does not have to finish one iteration before beginning the next.

The question here: what does the next iteration need from this one, how much sooner does the loop finish, and what does beginning early cost?

Work it out

L01 The loop of the Lecture 2 slides of MIT 6.S894 (11 September 2025): ld r3, mem[r0+r2], ld r4, mem[r1+r2], mul r3, r3, r4, add r5, r5, r3, addi r2, r2, 4, blt r2, $400, LOOP, and once the loop is left st addr[r6], r5; the first name of each instruction receives the result, r2 begins at 0 (assumed), and the loop does 100 iterations of 6 instructions, 601 instructions with the store. from: the Lecture 2 slides; r2 = 0 assumed

L02 DO An instruction needs another if it reads a register the other wrote. What do the two loads, the mul, the add and the addi of iteration 2 need from iteration 1? ____ . from: L01

CHECK 
loads of iteration 2: only iteration 1's addi (r2 = 4)
mul of iteration 2: only iteration 2's loads
add of iteration 2: its own mul and iteration 1's add (r5)
addi of iteration 2: only iteration 1's addi
two chains cross the iterations: addi to addi, and add to add; the loads hang from the first

L03 Timing assumed for this note: a load's value arrives 200 cycles after the load begins (the slides give DRAM as 100s of cycles); every other result is ready 1 cycle after its instruction begins; at most one instruction begins per cycle. from: assumed; the Lecture 2 slides

L04 DO Strictly in written order, cycles per iteration: ____ ; the whole loop: ____ . Beginning any ready instruction, at least ____ cycles (one instruction per cycle). A schedule of these rules computed for the exercise page gives 732. Ratio: ____ . from: L01, L02, L03; the schedule of page 20

CHECK 
in order: 2 loads (cycles 0, 1), mul at 201, add 202, addi 203, blt 204: 205 per iteration,
20,500 for 100, the store's result at 20,501
out of order: at least 601; computed 732; 20,501 / 732 = 28.0 times faster

L05 DO In the first 200 cycles only the loads, the addi and the blt can begin, 4 instructions per iteration. Load values waiting at once at cycle 199: ____ . Registers the program names for them: ____ . from: L02, L03

CHECK 
200 / 4 = 50 iterations' loads, 2 each: 100 values waiting, all named r3 or r4
the program names 2 registers; the processor needs 100 storage places for them,
which is called register renaming
