Split-k: pieces of one sum in different blocks

Topics: Lab 5, splitting work. A single idea, with a few lines to calculate. Each line uses only the lines above it, and the grey note names its source. Fill every ____ on paper, then open CHECK. Any unfamiliar word: words used on this site.

The idea

When the result of a matrix product is small and each of its numbers is a very long sum, one block per tile of the result leaves most of the GPU without work. The sum can be cut into pieces and the pieces given to more blocks, but then the pieces of one number belong to different blocks.

The question here: why must those pieces travel through memory and a second kernel, and what must the decision to split read?

Work it out

L01 The Lab 5 handout: the size 128 by 128 by 32768, with a target of 40% of the fastest possible. The Lab 4 handout: the course GPU has 48 SMs, the identical blocks of hardware the chip is built from, doing 26.7 TFLOP/s of multiply-adds together, and its memory delivers 360 GB/s. The CUDA programming guide: a block of threads resides on one SM, and blocks execute independently, in any order. The Lab 3 handout: launches issued one after another finish one before the next begins. from: the Lab 5, Lab 4 and Lab 3 handouts; the CUDA programming guide

L02 DO Blocks with 64 by 64 tiles: ____ ; multiply-adds per block ____ ; at one SM's 26.7 / 48 = 0.556 TFLOP/s, at least ____ µs. The bound for the size, from its 33,619,968 bytes at 360 GB/s: ____ µs; so at most ____ of the possible. from: L01

CHECK 
2 * 2 = 4 blocks; 64 * 64 = 4,096 numbers of C each, 32,768 products per number:
134,217,728 multiply-adds per block, 2 FLOP each: 483 µs
33,619,968 / 360 GB/s = 93.4 µs; 93.4 / 483 = 19%, under the 40% target

L03 DO With k cut into 16 pieces: blocks ____ . If every block stored its result straight into C, C would keep ____ . Why can no block wait for the others inside the launch? ____ . from: L01, L02

CHECK 
2 * 2 * 16 = 64 blocks, more than 48 SMs
one of the 16 pieces of each sum, whichever was stored last
blocks execute in any order, so no block can know that the others have even begun; each
piece goes to its own slice of a workspace, 16 * 128 * 128 * 4 = 1,048,576 bytes, and a
second launch adds the slices once the first has finished

L04 DO A decision written as total_blocks < 14 reads the laptop's 14 SMs. On the course GPU's 48, which of 128 by 128 by 32768 (4 blocks) and 256 by 256 by 8192 (16 blocks) would split? With 14: ____ . With 48: ____ . from: L01, L02

CHECK 
with 14: only 128 by 128 by 32768; with 48: both
cudaDeviceGetAttribute with cudaDevAttrMultiProcessorCount reads the number of SMs from
the GPU itself
