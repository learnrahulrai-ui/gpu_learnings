Bigger cores or more cores

Topics: Lecture 2, splitting work. A single idea, with a few lines to calculate. Each line uses only the lines above it, and the grey note names its source. Fill every ____ on paper, then open CHECK. Any unfamiliar word: words used on this site.

The idea

Lecture 2 plots CPU cores by size, counted in transistors, against the work each finishes per tick of its clock, next to a straight line on which work would grow in step with size. Beside the largest core the slide writes "3x off trend".

The question here: what does the 3 compare, and when does the same area do more work as one big core or as many small ones?

Work it out

L01 The slide "Diminishing returns to scaling single-core performance" of the Lecture 2 slides of MIT 6.S894 (11 September 2025): performance per clock from the Geekbench 6 composite, against core size at the same transistor size; the first core at 1x and 1x, the last, labelled Raptor Cove, at 12x size and 4x performance; a red straight line through the first core. from: the Lecture 2 slides

L02 DO The red line at 12x size: ____ . Raptor Cove: ____ . Ratio: ____ . Work per clock per unit of size, first core against Raptor Cove: ____ against ____ . from: L01

CHECK 
red line 12x; Raptor Cove 4x; 12 / 4 = 3, the 3 of "3x off trend"
1 against 4 / 12 = 0.333: each transistor of the big core does a third of the work

L03 DO In Raptor Cove's area, 12 small cores fit. Work split evenly 12 ways: ____ x against ____ x. Work that cannot be split: ____ x against ____ x. A job with share p split 12 ways takes (1 - p) + p / 12 clocks per unit on the small cores and 0.25 on Raptor Cove; equal at p = ____ . from: L01, L02

CHECK 
split: 12x against 4x, 3 times more; not split: 1x against 4x, the big core 4 times faster
(1 - p) + p / 12 = 0.25  ->  11p / 12 = 0.75  ->  p = 9 / 11 = 0.818
the 12 small cores finish first only when more than 82 percent of the job splits
