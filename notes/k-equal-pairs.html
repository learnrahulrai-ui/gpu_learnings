k-equal pairs and what a layout guarantees

Topics: Lab 6, memory. A single idea, with a few lines to calculate. Each line uses only the lines above it, and the grey note names its source. Fill every ____ on paper, then open CHECK. Any unfamiliar word: words used on this site.

The idea

Each number of a matrix product is a sum of products whose two numbers share the same k. A tensor core never reads k: it multiplies numbers in fixed places of registers.

The question here: what must a layout guarantee about k, and what happens when numbers change size between the load and the multiply?

Work it out

L01 C(m, n) = the sum over k of A(m, k) * B(k, n). NVIDIA's PTX manual: for the TF32 instruction m16n8k8, thread n holds A(g, t), A(g, t + 4) and B(t, g), B(t + 4, g); for the 16-bit m16n8k16, one register holds A(g, 2t) and A(g, 2t + 1); for the 8-bit m16n8k32, A(g, 4t) to A(g, 4t + 3). from: the lab starters; NVIDIA's PTX manual

L02 DO Of the 64 products of 8 numbers of A(m, .) and 8 of B(., n), how many enter C(m, n)? ____ . In the TF32 layout, which k do a thread's A and B numbers carry? ____ . from: L01

CHECK 
8, one for each k; 56 never enter
A(g, t), A(g, t + 4) and B(t, g), B(t + 4, g): both carry k = t and t + 4

L03 DO An 8-bit thread with t = 0 holds k = 0 to 3 in one register. After conversion to 16 bits, which threads must hold them? ____ . from: L01

CHECK 
the 16-bit layout puts k = 0, 1 in t = 0 and k = 2, 3 in t = 1: half must move to another
thread, which a conversion cannot do
